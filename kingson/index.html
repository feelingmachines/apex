Research notes

Feeling, vulnerability, and artificial agents

My current work, Skin in the Game, investigates how embodied vulnerability changes behavior. In simulation, agents’ energy and water reserves affect their ability to act. I study how they respond to threats and anticipate the bodily consequences of their choices. Learning useful models of those consequences remains an open problem.

Poster & agent videosTalk abstract2019 framework paper

Homeostasis, learning, and cooperation

I study how sharing internal states can support helping behavior between simulated agents, and how neural networks that regulate their own learning rates adapt when learned associations change.

Homeostatic couplingNeural network code

How the senses become a world

My doctoral and postdoctoral work used brain imaging and machine learning to study how the brain represents objects across senses. I also investigated how seeing an object changes our ability to hear it, and how the brain represents the meaning of stories across languages.

Short bio & portrait

Kingson Man is a neuroscientist and AI builder studying the connections between life, feeling, and intelligence. His work at Feeling Machines investigates homeostasis, vulnerability, and adaptive behavior in artificial agents. He earned his PhD in neuroscience at USC. His research has appeared in Nature Machine Intelligence and Science Robotics, and is featured in Michael Pollan’s A World Appears and Valerio Jalongo’s documentary Wider than the Sky.

Clinical AI at City of Hope

Separately from Feeling Machines, I lead applied AI projects in oncology at City of Hope. I’m a Data Science Manager, with a courtesy appointment as Associate Professor at the Beckman Research Institute.

Research

At City of Hope, I lead work on HopeLLM, a clinical AI platform for extracting and retrieving information from oncology records. We evaluate model outputs with clinicians on real cases. Projects include clinical trial matching and RESiN, a method designed to examine every note before answering a clinical question.
